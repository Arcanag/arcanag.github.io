01 / Why it exists

Nobody owned the seams.

Delivery builds and ships. Customer success owns the relationship. The work that falls between them — a client request, a slipped date, a disputed number — had no written owner, so it was settled by whoever argued longest.

Question 1

Whose job is this?

Settled by a one-question ownership test, not by seniority or by who noticed first.

Question 2

Where does a request go?

Settled by one point of entry, so nothing is agreed in a side channel and lost.

Question 3

Which number is right?

Settled by single-source-of-truth rules: one home for every artefact, everything else is a copy.

Drafted as v1.0 for leadership review · shown here as a reusable pattern, not as ratified company policy

An unwritten operating model is a recurring argument with extra steps.

Reference engagement: a portfolio of 3 accounts running ~4M calls a day at peak.

03 / The ownership test

One question. No debate.

Charters describe teams. The test decides a specific piece of work in seconds — and it has to be answerable by anyone, without a meeting.

The test

If this goes wrong in front of the client, who has to explain it?

Delivery owns it when…

The answer is about what was built, configured, deployed or measured — the product behaving as specified.

Customer success owns it when…

The answer is about expectations, priorities, commercials or the relationship — what the client was promised and told.

Illustrative wording · write the test in your own team’s language; the point is one question, applied the same way every time

05 / RACI-lite with turnaround

One owner. One clock.

A full RACI matrix is read once and ignored. RACI-lite keeps two columns that matter: who is accountable, and how fast it must move.

Illustrative RACI-lite rows: task, accountable team, consulted team, turnaround
TaskAccountableConsultedTurnaround
Log and acknowledge a new client requestCustomer successDeliverySame business day
Scope and estimate the requestDeliveryCustomer successAgreed per size
Commit a date to the clientCustomer successDeliveryAfter the estimate, never before
Production incident updateDeliveryCustomer successOn a fixed interval until closed
Weekly status to the clientCustomer successDeliveryWeekly

06 / Intake and source of truth

One way in. One place it lives.

Most “who said what” arguments are really two records of the same thing. Fix the entry point and the home, and the argument has nothing to stand on.

Single point of entry

Every request is logged before it is worked

Requests from calls, chats and email land in one intake queue with a source link. Work that never entered intake doesn’t exist yet.

Single source of truth

One home per artefact

Each tracker, spec and metric has exactly one authoritative home. Anything else is a copy, labelled as one, and loses to the source when they disagree.

Fight about it once, in writing — or fight about it weekly, forever.

07 / Account health

Three lenses. No surprises.

An account can be green on delivery and red on the relationship. Rating it through three separate lenses stops one good number hiding two bad ones.

Lens 1 · Delivery

Are commitments landing on time?

Dates kept, scope stable, no silent slips.

On track

Lens 2 · Performance

Is the product moving the client’s metric?

The outcome the client pays for, measured the way they measure it.

At risk

Lens 3 · Relationship

Does the client trust what we tell them?

Escalations, tone, responsiveness, sponsor engagement.

On track

08 / Cadence and escalation

A forum for everything. A ladder for the rest.

Recurring forums handle the expected. An escalation path handles the rest — with a trigger for each step, so climbing it is a rule, not a judgement call made under pressure.

09 / Adopting it

Easy to write. Hard to keep.

The model only works if it is used in the first argument after it is published. Four ways it fails:

  1. 01

    Writing content before structure

    Agree the seven pieces first. The wording inside them is the part worth arguing about — once.

  2. 02

    Exceptions on day one

    The first request that skips intake “because it’s urgent” teaches everyone that intake is optional.

  3. 03

    Averaging the health lenses

    One blended colour hides exactly the lens that is about to escalate.

  4. 04

    Calling a draft a policy

    Run it on a reference engagement, review it with leadership, then ratify. Skipping review buys compliance, not agreement.

Steal the structure. The content is yours to fight about once, in writing.
