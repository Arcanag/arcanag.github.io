01 / The first agents

Voice bots were agents first.

Before “agentic AI” was a buzzword, voice bots did agent work in production — multi-turn systems handling real phone calls for banks, telecoms and insurers.

PerceiveRecognise intent from noisy, accented, interrupted speech.
ExtractPull account numbers, dates and amounts out of natural conversation, in real time.
RememberKeep context across turns — what the caller said three turns ago.
DecideChoose when to act alone and when to transfer to a human.
Respond fastOn a phone call, a 2-second pause feels like an eternity.

Perceive, reason, act, escalate — the same loop every agent runs.

02 / Confidence thresholds

The score matters less than what you do with it.

Every utterance gets a confidence score. The thresholds took years to tune — and they scale with the cost of being wrong.

High confidence

> 0.85

Act on the intent

Medium confidence

0.5–0.85

Confirm before acting

Low confidence

< 0.5

Escalate to a human

Rule of thumbA $500 payment confirms at 0.9. A balance lookup is fine at 0.7.

03 / Graceful degradation

The fallback is the product.

Voice bots can’t crash — there’s a person on the line. So every bot has a ladder that narrows towards a human, one step at a time.

  1. Step 1

    Rephrase and retry

    “Could you say that differently?”

  2. Step 2

    Offer options

    “I can help with payments, balance enquiries or account updates.”

  3. Step 3

    Transfer to a human

    “Let me connect you with someone who can help.”

Worse

80% handled perfectly, 20% crash

Better

70% handled perfectly, 30% escalated gracefully

Users don’t mind talking to an AI. They mind being stuck with one.

The quality of your escalation path matters as much as the quality of your automation.

04 / Patterns everywhere

Same patterns. New clothes.

When I built Agentic PM, I didn’t invent these patterns. I ported them from voice AI.

Voice bot patternbecomesAgentic AI pattern
Confidence scoring (NLU threshold)→Task confidence — flag low-certainty tasks for review
Fallback chains (rephrase → options → transfer)→Routing fallback — ask a clarifying question before misrouting
Human escalation (transfer to a live agent)→Human review — sensitivity-gated approval before sending
Containment rate (% of calls resolved by AI)→Autonomy rate (% of tasks done without intervention)

05 / Containment rate

One metric for every agent.

Containment — or its equivalent — should be the north-star metric for any AI agent system, because it captures capability and calibration together.

Voice bots% of calls resolved by AI
Chatbots% of tickets deflected without a human agent
Coding assistants% of suggestions accepted without modification
Agent systems% of tasks completed without human intervention
AI PM tools% of planning decisions that aren’t overridden
95%
autonomous — but 20% wrong

Looks better on a dashboard. Delivers less.

70%
autonomous — 98% accurate

The higher effective containment rate.

Watch outHigher isn’t always better. Push containment from 70% to 90% by making transfers harder, and CSAT drops.

06 / What agents haven’t learned

Solved problems, repeated.

Across AutoGPT, CrewAI and LangGraph, I keep seeing mistakes voice AI made and fixed years ago.

CapabilityVoice AIMost agents
Escape hatches — hand off to a human mid-taskSolvedMissing
Error recovery — back out gracefully when context goes wrongSolvedMissing
Capability transparency — declare limits up frontSolvedMissing
Latency matched to the taskSolvedMissing

A judgement from following the ecosystem, not a benchmark.

Most agent demos run until they succeed or fail, with no mid-task escape hatch. Users discover limits through failure. And agents often take 30 seconds over a decision a human makes in 2 — perceived intelligence is a function of speed and accuracy.

If you’re building agents, study voice bots first.

The patterns for confidence, fallback, escalation and containment are battle-tested across millions of production interactions. If you’ve built voice, chat or dialogue systems, you already understand the hardest parts of agent design.
