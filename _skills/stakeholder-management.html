02 / The escalation

Decompose, don’t defend.

A banking client escalated: promise-to-pay on a flagship LLM flow had fallen from 5.03% to 3.03%. Instead of defending the bot, I decomposed the decline — two causes, neither of them the bot.

17%
voicemail share of “engaged”
→
30%
after a voicemail surge

Denominator change. Voicemail inflated the “engaged” base and deflated every rate below it, with nothing changing in the bot.

4.37
attempts per lead
→
3.51
after a cadence change

Configuration change. Fewer dial attempts per lead — a setting, not a performance drop.

  1. 01

    Freeze the definitions

    Agree what each funnel stage means before arguing about its number.

  2. 02

    Separate denominator from numerator

    Most “drops” are a changed base, not a worse outcome.

  3. 03

    Replay it from their raw data

    Each factor quantified, given an owner and a fix, on a timeline the client could follow.

The escalation ended because the client could follow every step in their own data.

03 / The handover

New account. Zero dropped blockers.

A global smartphone brand’s account moved to me mid-quarter. An incoming checklist did the work a goodbye call can’t.

Before exit

One hard blocker

The checklist surfaced the blocker the outgoing PM was still carrying — escalated while they were still there to explain it.

Week one

A slip, said early

A model-training timeline slip went to the client before they asked, not after.

Before the review

An incident, root-caused

An upstream data-sync change was zeroing a client dashboard. Fixed before it reached a monthly business review.

Trust restored

The conversation shifted from “we don’t trust your metrics” to “show us how to improve them.”
